# Accent Track 1 — Prosody (rhythm, stress, intonation)

Rhythm is the single biggest thing that marks an accent — bigger than any consonant. Malayalam/Indian English gives every syllable near-equal weight; General American is **stress-timed**: strong beats on the important words, and the small words squeezed into a quick "uh" (schwa). Fix this and you sound dramatically less marked, with zero audio needed. Each card is a 20–40 second micro-game.

Notation: **CAPS** = stressed/strong syllable. _uh_ = reduced to schwa. • = a beat.

## PR01. The de-DUM heartbeat
**The tell:** You give every syllable equal length, so speech sounds even and "machine-gun" — the metronome that screams non-native.
**GenAm move:** Pick a few strong beats per phrase and let everything between them shrink; the gap between beats stays roughly even, not the syllables.
**Notation:** • i'd **LIKE** _tuh_ **GO** _tuh_ _thuh_ **STORE** • (3 strong beats, the rest squeezed)
**Micro-game (20s):** Tap the table only on LIKE, GO, STORE and say it 3×, keeping your taps evenly spaced no matter how many tiny words fall between them.

## PR02. Content strong, function weak
**The tell:** You stress small grammar words (the, of, to, is, and, can) as loudly as the real words.
**GenAm move:** Punch only the **content** words — nouns, main verbs, adjectives, adverbs; let **function** words (articles, prepositions, pronouns, helpers) go soft and fast.
**Notation:** _thuh_ **CAT** _is_ _on_ _thuh_ **TABLE** _in_ _thuh_ **KITCHEN**
**Micro-game (20s):** Whisper every weak word and shout every CAPS word in the line above, twice — exaggerate the size difference.

## PR03. Schwa: reduce to "uh"
**The tell:** You say full clear vowels in the, a, of, to, for ("aae", "tooo", "for") instead of a lazy _uh_.
**GenAm move:** In unstressed spots these collapse: the→_thuh_, a→_uh_, of→_uhv_, to→_tuh_, for→_fer_. The vowel goes to a neutral mumble.
**Notation:** _uh_ **CUP** _uhv_ **TEA** _fer_ **TWO** _uhv_ **US**
**Micro-game (20s):** Say "a cup of tea for two of us" slowly, then again replacing every small word's vowel with a relaxed _uh_ — feel your jaw barely move on them.

## PR04. Crushing "to" and "and"
**The tell:** You keep "to" and "and" as crisp separate words, breaking the flow.
**GenAm move:** They glue onto neighbors: want to→_wanna_, going to→_gonna_, fish and chips→_fish'n chips_, rock and roll→_rock'n roll_. (Spoken rhythm, not spelling.)
**Notation:** _i_ **WANNA** **GO** • **FISH** _'n_ **CHIPS** • **ROCK** _'n_ **ROLL**
**Micro-game (20s):** Say "I want to go" three times, getting faster each time until "want to" becomes one smooshed _wanna_ beat.

## PR05. Word stress: where the beat lands
**The tell:** You put even stress across a word, or stress the wrong syllable, so the word's shape sounds off.
**GenAm move:** Each word has ONE main beat — and it can move: nouns vs verbs often flip. Learn the shape, not just the sounds.
**Notation:** **PHO**-_tuh_-graph • _phuh_-**TOG**-_ruh_-phy • a **RE**-_cord_ (noun) vs _tuh_ re-**CORD** (verb)
**Micro-game (20s):** Say "PHOto / phoTOGraphy" back-to-back 3×, sliding the beat forward; then "REcord (the noun) / reCORD (the verb)" 3×.

## PR06. Stress in longer words
**The tell:** On big words you spread the energy out, landing flat or on the wrong syllable.
**GenAm move:** Find the one loud syllable and let the rest reduce to schwa around it.
**Notation:** _duh_-**VEL**-_uhp_-_m…uhnt_ • im-**POR**-_tuhnt_ • **NE**-_cuh_-_sair_-y
**Micro-game (20s):** Hum just the rhythm of each word (da-DA-da-da) first, then drop the syllables in: development, important, necessary — 2× each.

## PR07. Sentence stress: the last big word wins
**The tell:** You end sentences flat, or stress an early word, so nothing signals "this is the point."
**GenAm move:** The **tonic** (biggest beat) normally lands on the **last content word** of the group. That word gets length, pitch, and volume.
**Notation:** _i_ _left_ _my_ **KEYS** _in_ _the_ **CAR**
**Micro-game (20s):** Say the line and physically lean forward on CAR — make it the longest, highest word. Repeat 3×.

## PR08. Contrastive stress
**The tell:** You stress the default word even when you mean to correct or contrast something.
**GenAm move:** Move the big beat onto the word that carries the contrast — it overrides the normal "last word" rule.
**Notation:** _i_ _said_ _the_ **RED** _one_, _not_ _the_ **BLUE** _one_
**Micro-game (20s):** Say it three ways, moving the punch each time: "**I** said…" / "I **said**…" / "the **RED** one" — feel the meaning shift.

## PR09. Thought groups (chunk + pause)
**The tell:** You pause in random spots (or never), often after small words, so phrasing sounds choppy.
**GenAm move:** Break speech into meaning chunks; pause only at the slashes, ride smoothly through each chunk.
**Notation:** _when_ _i_ **GET** **HOME** / _i'll_ **CALL** _you_ / _and_ _we'll_ **TALK**
**Micro-game (20s):** Draw a slash where each chunk ends, then say it taking a tiny breath only at each slash — 2×.

## PR10. Statements & wh-questions fall
**The tell:** You let the pitch stay flat or rise at the end of statements, which sounds uncertain or "listy."
**GenAm move:** Statements and **wh-questions** (what/where/when/why/how) glide **down** on the tonic word — a confident drop.
**Notation:** _where_ _did_ _you_ **PARK** ↘ • _i_ _put_ _it_ _on_ _the_ **DESK** ↘
**Micro-game (20s):** Say each line and drop your hand down through the air on the last word, like landing a plane. 3×.

## PR11. Yes/no questions rise
**The tell:** You use the same falling tune for yes/no questions, so they don't sound like questions.
**GenAm move:** Questions answerable by "yes/no" glide **up** at the end — pitch lifts on the tonic.
**Notation:** _are_ _you_ **COMING** ↗ • _did_ _you_ _eat_ **YET** ↗
**Micro-game (20s):** Say each line and raise your hand upward on the last word; do it once falling (wrong) then rising (right) to feel the difference.

## PR12. List & continuation rise-then-fall
**The tell:** You drop your pitch on every list item, making each sound like a finished sentence.
**GenAm move:** **Rise** on each item to signal "more coming," then **fall** on the final one to signal "done."
**Notation:** _eggs_ ↗ , _milk_ ↗ , _bread_ ↗ , _and_ **CHEESE** ↘
**Micro-game (20s):** Count "one ↗, two ↗, three ↗, four ↘" with rising hand on each, dropping only on the last — then swap in eggs/milk/bread/cheese.

**🔊 Optional model:** type the sentence into a US text-to-speech and mimic once.
