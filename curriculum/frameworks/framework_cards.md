# Conversation Framework Cards

Each framework is a sentence engine — a fixed opening that lets you start speaking before you have planned the whole sentence. Learn one in under 60 seconds: read it, say both examples aloud, then build one new sentence of your own. The goal is speed of starting, not memorizing.

### Opinion

## F001. I think…
**Use:** When you want to share your view without sounding too forceful.
**Examples:** I think we should test this before the demo. / I think the second design looks cleaner.
**Drill:** Say one thing you think about your current project.

## F002. In my opinion…
**Use:** When you want to clearly mark something as your personal view.
**Examples:** In my opinion, the meeting could have been an email. / In my opinion, mornings are better for deep work.
**Drill:** Give your opinion on a movie you watched recently.

## F003. From my experience…
**Use:** When your point comes from things you've actually done.
**Examples:** From my experience, users skip long forms. / From my experience, weekend deployments cause stress.
**Drill:** Share one lesson from your work, starting with this frame.

### Stating a point

## F004. The main point is…
**Use:** When you want to cut to the core of what matters.
**Examples:** The main point is that we missed the deadline. / The main point is keeping the customer happy.
**Drill:** State the main point of your day in one sentence.

## F005. The problem is…
**Use:** When you need to name the obstacle clearly.
**Examples:** The problem is that the server keeps crashing. / The problem is we don't have enough time.
**Drill:** Name one problem you're facing right now.

## F006. The best option is…
**Use:** When you're recommending the strongest choice among many.
**Examples:** The best option is to launch next week instead. / The best option is to hire one more person.
**Drill:** Pick a decision and say what the best option is.

## F007. It depends on…
**Use:** When the answer isn't fixed and varies with conditions.
**Examples:** It depends on how much budget we have. / It depends on whether the client agrees.
**Drill:** Answer a question with "It depends on…" and one condition.

### Explaining

## F008. What I mean is…
**Use:** When you want to make your point clearer or rephrase it.
**Examples:** What I mean is we should slow down, not stop. / What I mean is the design needs more contrast.
**Drill:** Say something, then restart it with "What I mean is…".

## F009. Let me explain…
**Use:** When you're about to give the background or reasoning.
**Examples:** Let me explain why the report was late. / Let me explain how this feature works.
**Drill:** Pick a topic and start explaining it with this frame.

## F010. For example…
**Use:** When you want to back up a point with a concrete case.
**Examples:** For example, last month we lost two clients. / For example, you can press Ctrl+S to save.
**Drill:** Make a claim, then give a "For example…" to support it.

## F011. The reason is…
**Use:** When you want to give the cause behind something.
**Examples:** The reason is the supplier raised their prices. / The reason is I had a doctor's appointment.
**Drill:** State a decision, then explain "The reason is…".

### Reasoning & contrast

## F012. That's why…
**Use:** When you want to connect a cause to its result.
**Examples:** That's why I came in early today. / That's why we switched to the new tool.
**Drill:** Mention a cause, then add the result with "That's why…".

## F013. However…
**Use:** When you want to add a contrasting point.
**Examples:** However, the price is still too high for us. / However, the team finished it on time.
**Drill:** State something, then add a contrast with "However…".

## F014. On the other hand…
**Use:** When you want to present the opposite side of an argument.
**Examples:** On the other hand, working remotely saves commute time. / On the other hand, the cheaper plan has fewer features.
**Drill:** Give one view, then the other side with this frame.

## F015. Even though…
**Use:** When something is true despite an obstacle.
**Examples:** Even though it rained, we still finished the site visit. / Even though I was tired, I joined the call.
**Drill:** Make a sentence with "Even though…" about today.

## F016. Compared to…
**Use:** When you want to measure one thing against another.
**Examples:** Compared to last year, our sales doubled. / Compared to the old app, this one is much faster.
**Drill:** Compare two things you use with this frame.

### Suggesting & deciding

## F017. I suggest…
**Use:** When you want to offer an idea lightly.
**Examples:** I suggest we take a short break now. / I suggest testing it on a small group first.
**Drill:** Suggest one improvement for your team.

## F018. I recommend…
**Use:** When you want to advise something with confidence.
**Examples:** I recommend reading the contract twice. / I recommend the window seat for that flight.
**Drill:** Recommend a book, tool, or place using this frame.

## F019. We should consider…
**Use:** When you want the group to weigh an option.
**Examples:** We should consider moving the deadline. / We should consider a different vendor.
**Drill:** Raise one option your team should think about.

## F020. We need to decide…
**Use:** When a choice has to be made and you want to push for it.
**Examples:** We need to decide who leads the project. / We need to decide the budget by Friday.
**Drill:** Name one decision your team needs to make.

## F021. I'd prefer…
**Use:** When you want to state your preference politely.
**Examples:** I'd prefer to meet in the morning. / I'd prefer tea over coffee, actually.
**Drill:** State a preference between two options.

### Agreeing & disagreeing

## F022. I agree with…
**Use:** When you want to support someone's point.
**Examples:** I agree with your plan to launch early. / I agree with what Priya said about the budget.
**Drill:** Agree with one thing someone said today.

## F023. I disagree with…
**Use:** When you want to push back clearly but respectfully.
**Examples:** I disagree with cutting the testing phase. / I disagree with that price estimate.
**Drill:** Disagree with a common opinion using this frame.

## F024. I partly agree…
**Use:** When you accept some of an idea but not all of it.
**Examples:** I partly agree, but the timeline worries me. / I partly agree; the plan needs more detail.
**Drill:** Partly agree with a statement, then add your reservation.

## F025. That makes sense…
**Use:** When you want to show you follow and accept the logic.
**Examples:** That makes sense, let's go with it. / That makes sense now that you explained it.
**Drill:** React to an explanation with "That makes sense…".

## F026. I see it differently…
**Use:** When you want to offer another view without conflict.
**Examples:** I see it differently; I think users want fewer steps. / I see it differently, but I get your point.
**Drill:** Give a different view on something with this frame.

### Clarifying & repairing

## F027. I'm not sure…
**Use:** When you want to admit uncertainty honestly.
**Examples:** I'm not sure that's the right number. / I'm not sure I can finish it today.
**Drill:** Express doubt about one plan using this frame.

## F028. Could you clarify…?
**Use:** When you need someone to make something clearer.
**Examples:** Could you clarify which file you mean? / Could you clarify what the deadline is?
**Drill:** Ask a real clarifying question ending in "?".

## F029. Do you mean…?
**Use:** When you want to check you understood correctly.
**Examples:** Do you mean the Friday report or the Monday one? / Do you mean we should cancel the trip?
**Drill:** Restate someone's point as a "Do you mean…?" question.

## F030. Just to confirm…
**Use:** When you want to lock in shared understanding.
**Examples:** Just to confirm, the meeting is at 3 PM. / Just to confirm, you'll send the invoice today?
**Drill:** Confirm one detail of a plan with this frame.

## F031. Let me rephrase that…
**Use:** When your first attempt came out badly and you want a do-over.
**Examples:** Let me rephrase that, I didn't mean it that way. / Let me rephrase that more simply.
**Drill:** Say a clumsy sentence, then fix it with this frame.

## F032. Correct me if I'm wrong…
**Use:** When you want to state something but stay open to correction.
**Examples:** Correct me if I'm wrong, the launch is next Tuesday. / Correct me if I'm wrong, you handle the south region?
**Drill:** State an assumption and invite correction with this frame.

### Work & status

## F033. The priority is…
**Use:** When you want to flag what matters most right now.
**Examples:** The priority is fixing the login bug. / The priority is getting client approval.
**Drill:** Name your top priority for the week.

## F034. The deadline is…
**Use:** When you need to state when something is due.
**Examples:** The deadline is this Friday at noon. / The deadline is the end of the month.
**Drill:** State a real deadline you have right now.

## F035. The next step is…
**Use:** When you want to move a plan forward.
**Examples:** The next step is to share it with the client. / The next step is to test on real devices.
**Drill:** Say the next step in something you're working on.

## F036. I'm working on…
**Use:** When you want to report what's in progress.
**Examples:** I'm working on the quarterly report. / I'm working on improving my pronunciation.
**Drill:** Say what you're working on today.

## F037. I've finished…
**Use:** When you want to report something is done.
**Examples:** I've finished the first draft of the proposal. / I've finished testing the payment flow.
**Drill:** Report one thing you've finished recently.

## F038. I'm blocked on…
**Use:** When something is stopping your progress.
**Examples:** I'm blocked on the design approval. / I'm blocked on access to the database.
**Drill:** Name one thing you're blocked on right now.

### Narration & time

## F039. Recently…
**Use:** When you want to talk about the near past.
**Examples:** Recently, I started learning to cook. / Recently, our team grew to ten people.
**Drill:** Share one recent change in your life.

## F040. These days…
**Use:** When you describe an ongoing current situation.
**Examples:** These days, I work mostly from home. / These days, everyone uses video calls.
**Drill:** Describe a current habit with "These days…".

## F041. At first…
**Use:** When you want to begin telling how something started.
**Examples:** At first, I found the job difficult. / At first, the app kept crashing.
**Drill:** Start a short story about a change with this frame.

## F042. After that…
**Use:** When you want to continue a sequence of events.
**Examples:** After that, we moved to a bigger office. / After that, the issue disappeared.
**Drill:** Add the next event in a story with "After that…".

## F043. In the end…
**Use:** When you want to give the outcome of a story.
**Examples:** In the end, the client signed the deal. / In the end, we chose the simpler option.
**Drill:** Finish a short story with "In the end…".

## F044. I used to…
**Use:** When you describe a past habit that has changed.
**Examples:** I used to commute two hours every day. / I used to be afraid of speaking up.
**Drill:** Say one thing you used to do but no longer do.

### Importance & ability

## F045. It's important to…
**Use:** When you want to stress what matters.
**Examples:** It's important to reply to clients quickly. / It's important to take breaks during the day.
**Drill:** Say one thing it's important to do at work.

## F046. It's better to…
**Use:** When you want to recommend the wiser choice.
**Examples:** It's better to ask than to guess. / It's better to launch small and improve later.
**Drill:** Give advice with "It's better to…".

## F047. I'm trying to…
**Use:** When you want to describe an effort in progress.
**Examples:** I'm trying to speak more slowly and clearly. / I'm trying to finish this by tonight.
**Drill:** Say one thing you're trying to do these days.

## F048. I'm able to…
**Use:** When you want to state what you can do.
**Examples:** I'm able to handle the client calls now. / I'm able to join the meeting after lunch.
**Drill:** State one new thing you're able to do.

### Summarizing

## F049. To summarize…
**Use:** When you want to wrap up a longer point.
**Examples:** To summarize, we're on track but slightly over budget. / To summarize, the plan works with small changes.
**Drill:** Summarize your day in one sentence.

## F050. In short…
**Use:** When you want to give a quick bottom line.
**Examples:** In short, we need more time. / In short, the customer is happy.
**Drill:** Give the short version of a long story.

## F051. Overall…
**Use:** When you want to give a general judgment.
**Examples:** Overall, the event went well. / Overall, the team did a great job.
**Drill:** Give an overall verdict on something you tried.

## F052. The point is…
**Use:** When you want to drive home your core message.
**Examples:** The point is we can't keep delaying. / The point is the customer comes first.
**Drill:** State the point you most want someone to remember.

### Questions

## F053. What do you think…?
**Use:** When you want to invite someone's opinion.
**Examples:** What do you think about moving the date? / What do you think of this design?
**Drill:** Ask someone for their view with this frame.

## F054. What's the next step…?
**Use:** When you want to push a plan forward as a question.
**Examples:** What's the next step on the contract? / What's the next step after testing?
**Drill:** Ask about the next step in a project.

## F055. What's blocking this…?
**Use:** When you want to find out what's holding something up.
**Examples:** What's blocking this from going live? / What's blocking this approval?
**Drill:** Ask what's blocking one of your tasks.

## F056. How can I help…?
**Use:** When you want to offer support.
**Examples:** How can I help with the launch? / How can I help you finish on time?
**Drill:** Offer help to a colleague using this frame.
