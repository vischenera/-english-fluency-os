# Accent Track 2 — The Segmental Tells

You don't need to fix every sound — listeners place an accent from a handful of features. These are the highest-impact ones for a Malayalam/Indian speaker moving toward General American. Each card is a 20–40 second micro-drill: read the fix, do the minimal pair, optionally pull a model and mimic once.

How to hear a model (no files needed): search the word on **YouGlish** (set accent to US) for real native clips, **Forvo** for single words, or type it into any **US text-to-speech**. Then record yourself and compare.

## SG01. Retroflex T/D → alveolar T/D
**The tell:** You curl your tongue tip back to the roof for T and D, giving the hard "Indian" ʈ/ɖ — the single biggest accent flag.
**GenAm move:** Put your tongue tip flat on the little bump just behind your upper front teeth (the alveolar ridge). No curling back. Tap and release — light, forward, front of the mouth.
**Say it:** /t/ /d/ at the ridge → "time, today, water, ladder"
**Minimal pair (20s):** Say "today" your usual way, then with the tip flat and forward — alternate 3× and feel the tongue move forward each time.
**🔊 Model:** Search "water" on YouGlish (US) or a US text-to-speech, mimic once, then record & compare.

## SG02. Aspiration of P/T/K
**The tell:** Your P/T/K at the start of stressed syllables come out "clean," with no breath — so "pin" sounds clipped, almost like "bin."
**GenAm move:** Release a small puff of air right after the consonant. Hold a tissue an inch from your lips: on "pin / top / key" the tissue should flutter. P/T/K start stressed words with breath.
**Say it:** pʰ tʰ kʰ → "pin, top, key, paper"
**Minimal pair (20s):** "spin" (no puff) vs "pin" (puff) — say each 3× with the tissue and watch it move only on "pin."
**🔊 Model:** Search "paper" on YouGlish (US) or a US text-to-speech, mimic once, then record & compare.

## SG03. V vs W
**The tell:** You merge V and W into one in-between sound (ʋ), so "vet" and "wet" come out the same.
**GenAm move:** For **V**: rest your top teeth lightly on your bottom lip and turn the voice on (buzzing). For **W**: no teeth at all — round your lips like a tiny kiss and glide. Teeth = V, lips = W.
**Say it:** /v/ (teeth+buzz) and /w/ (round lips) → "vet, vine, very" vs "wet, wine, wary"
**Minimal pair (20s):** "vet / wet" — say each 3×, feel top teeth touch lip on the V and disappear on the W.
**🔊 Model:** Search "very" on YouGlish (US) or a US text-to-speech, mimic once, then record & compare.

## SG04. TH /θ/ (think)
**The tell:** You swap the breathy TH for a hard "t" (or sometimes "s"), so "think" becomes "tink."
**GenAm move:** Push your tongue tip lightly between your top and bottom front teeth and blow air through — no voice, just a soft hiss of breath. Pull the tongue back to finish the word.
**Say it:** /θ/ (tongue between teeth, air) → "think, three, both, month"
**Minimal pair (20s):** "tink / think" — say each 3×, stick the tongue tip out for "think" and feel air over it.
**🔊 Model:** Search "think" on YouGlish (US) or a US text-to-speech, mimic once, then record & compare.

## SG05. TH /ð/ (this)
**The tell:** You replace the voiced TH with a hard "d," so "this" becomes "dis."
**GenAm move:** Same tongue position as SG04 — tip lightly between the teeth — but now turn your voice on so it buzzes. It should feel like a soft vibrating hum, not a sharp "d."
**Say it:** /ð/ (tongue between teeth, voiced) → "this, that, mother, they"
**Minimal pair (20s):** "dis / this" — say each 3×, let the tongue peek out and buzz on "this."
**🔊 Model:** Search "mother" on YouGlish (US) or a US text-to-speech, mimic once, then record & compare.

## SG06. GenAm rhotic R
**The tell:** You roll or tap the R, and you drop the R after vowels (so "car" and "work" lose their R).
**GenAm move:** Never tap or roll. Bunch the tongue up and back (or curl the tip slightly) so the whole sound gets an "r-colored" growl — tongue tip touches nothing. Crucially, **keep the R after a vowel**: "car" ends with the R still sounding.
**Say it:** /r/ and /ɚ/ (no tap, hold the R) → "right, car, work, four"
**Minimal pair (20s):** "caa" (dropped) vs "car" (held R) — say each 3×, growl the R to the very end of "car."
**🔊 Model:** Search "work" on YouGlish (US) or a US text-to-speech, mimic once, then record & compare.

## SG07. Diphthongs, not monophthongs
**The tell:** You flatten gliding vowels into a single steady tone — "day" sounds like "deh," "go" like "goh."
**GenAm move:** Let the vowel move. FACE /eɪ/: start "eh" and slide up to "ee." GOAT /oʊ/: start "oh" and round the lips toward "oo." PRICE /aɪ/: start open "ah" and slide to "ee." Two positions in one vowel.
**Say it:** /eɪ/ "say, day" · /oʊ/ "go, no" · /aɪ/ "my, time"
**Minimal pair (20s):** "deh / day" — say each 3×, let your jaw and lips visibly move on the gliding "day."
**🔊 Model:** Search "day" on YouGlish (US) or a US text-to-speech, mimic once, then record & compare.

## SG08. The TRAP vowel /æ/
**The tell:** You raise /æ/ toward /e/, so "bad" drifts near "bed" and "man" near "men."
**GenAm move:** Drop your jaw open wider and spread the lips slightly, tongue low and forward — it's a brighter, flatter "aaa." Open more than feels natural; keep it clearly below "bed."
**Say it:** /æ/ (jaw open, flat) → "cat, bad, man"
**Minimal pair (20s):** "bed / bad" — say each 3×, drop the jaw lower on "bad" and feel it open.
**🔊 Model:** Search "bad" on YouGlish (US) or a US text-to-speech, mimic once, then record & compare.

## SG09. Dark L vs clear L
**The tell:** You use one bright, forward "clear" L everywhere, so "ball" and "cool" sound thin and front-of-mouth.
**GenAm move:** Two L's. **Clear L** before a vowel ("light"): tip up front, sound bright. **Dark L** at the end of a word ("full, ball, cool"): keep the tip up but pull the **back** of the tongue up and back so the L sounds deep and swallowed — almost an "ull/ool."
**Say it:** clear /l/ "light" vs dark /ɫ/ "full, ball, cool"
**Minimal pair (20s):** "light / ball" — say each 3×, feel L bright and forward on "light," deep and back on "ball."
**🔊 Model:** Search "ball" on YouGlish (US) or a US text-to-speech, mimic once, then record & compare.

## SG10. Word-final consonants & clusters
**The tell:** At the end of a word you either add a vowel ("asked-uh") or drop the last sound ("ask," "nex"), so consonant clusters collapse.
**GenAm move:** Land every final consonant cleanly, stacked with no extra vowel between or after them. Say the cluster slowly first, then at speed: "ask-t," "neks-t," "help-t" — all the sounds, nothing added.
**Say it:** keep the final cluster → "asked /æskt/, next /nɛkst/, helped /hɛlpt/"
**Minimal pair (20s):** "ask / asked" — say each 3×, snap the "-kt" on cleanly with no "uh" after it.
**🔊 Model:** Search "asked" on YouGlish (US) or a US text-to-speech, mimic once, then record & compare.
