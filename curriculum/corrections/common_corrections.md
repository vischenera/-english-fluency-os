# Common Correction Cards

These are the highest-frequency Malayalam/Indian-English transfer errors — the ones that most mark speech as non-native. Learn the *pattern* behind each, not just the sentence. One card takes under 60 seconds: say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then build one new sentence.

## C001. discuss about → discuss
**Why:** "Discuss" is already transitive and carries the meaning of "talk about," so adding "about" is redundant.
**Examples:** Let's discuss the budget tomorrow. / We discussed the new plan for an hour.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C002. am having a car → have a car
**Why:** "Have" for possession is a stative verb, so it isn't used in the continuous. Reserve "having" for activities like having lunch or having a meeting.
**Examples:** I have a car. / She has two brothers.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C003. one of my friend → one of my friends
**Why:** "One of" picks a single item from a group, so the noun that follows must be plural.
**Examples:** One of my friends lives in Dubai. / He is one of the best players on the team.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C004. I am agree → I agree
**Why:** "Agree" is a verb, not an adjective, so it doesn't take the linking verb "am." You simply conjugate it directly.
**Examples:** I agree with you. / We agreed on a price.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C005. I didn't went → I didn't go
**Why:** After the auxiliary "did/didn't," the main verb stays in its base form; the past tense is already carried by "did."
**Examples:** I didn't go to the party. / She didn't call me back.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C006. She don't know → She doesn't know
**Why:** Third-person singular (he/she/it) takes "doesn't," not "don't," in the present tense.
**Examples:** She doesn't know the answer. / He doesn't eat meat.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C007. He go to office → He goes to the office
**Why:** Third-person singular adds "-s" to the verb ("goes"), and a specific workplace usually needs the article "the."
**Examples:** He goes to the office at nine. / She goes to the gym every morning.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C008. I am working here since 2020 → I have been working here since 2020
**Why:** An action that started in the past and continues now uses the present perfect continuous, not the simple present.
**Examples:** I have been working here since 2020. / They have been living in Chennai since last year.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C009. I know him from 5 years → I have known him for 5 years
**Why:** Duration up to now takes the present perfect with "for" (a length of time), not "from."
**Examples:** I have known him for five years. / We have been married for ten years.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C010. Yesterday I have gone → Yesterday I went
**Why:** A finished time word like "yesterday" requires the simple past, not the present perfect, which is for unspecified time.
**Examples:** Yesterday I went to the market. / Last week she finished the project.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C011. I said him → I told him
**Why:** "Tell" takes a person as its object ("told him"); "say" does not — you say something to someone.
**Examples:** I told him the truth. / She told me a story.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C012. Explain me → Explain to me
**Why:** "Explain" cannot take a person as a direct object; the person needs the preposition "to."
**Examples:** Explain it to me. / She explained the rules to the new staff.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C013. Can you suggest me → Can you suggest a solution
**Why:** "Suggest" cannot be followed directly by a person; you suggest a thing, or suggest something to someone.
**Examples:** Can you suggest a good restaurant? / He suggested a different approach to me.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C014. married with → married to
**Why:** The fixed collocation is "married to" someone, not "married with."
**Examples:** She is married to a doctor. / He got married to his college sweetheart.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C015. different than → different from
**Why:** In standard usage, two things are "different from" each other; "than" is for comparatives like "bigger than."
**Examples:** This phone is different from the old one. / Her style is very different from mine.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C016. enter into the room → enter the room
**Why:** "Enter" is transitive when it means walk into a place, so it needs no preposition. ("Enter into" is only for agreements or negotiations.)
**Examples:** He entered the room quietly. / Please enter the building through the side door.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C017. return back → return
**Why:** "Return" already means to come or go back, so "back" is redundant.
**Examples:** I will return on Monday. / Please return the book by Friday.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C018. repeat again → repeat
**Why:** "Repeat" already means to do or say something again, so "again" is redundant.
**Examples:** Could you repeat that, please? / She repeated the instructions slowly.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C019. revert back → reply / get back
**Why:** "Revert" means to return to a previous state, not to reply. To answer someone, say "reply" or "get back to you."
**Examples:** I will reply by this evening. / Let me get back to you tomorrow.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C020. today morning → this morning
**Why:** English uses "this morning / this afternoon / this evening," not "today morning."
**Examples:** I called you this morning. / We have a meeting this afternoon.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C021. yesterday night → last night
**Why:** The fixed phrase is "last night," not "yesterday night."
**Examples:** I slept badly last night. / Last night we watched a film.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C022. I have a doubt → I have a question
**Why:** A "doubt" means uncertainty or disbelief; when you want something clarified, the natural word is "question."
**Examples:** I have a question about the schedule. / Can I ask a quick question?
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C023. cope up with → cope with
**Why:** The verb is "cope with"; there is no "up" in the phrase.
**Examples:** She copes well with pressure. / How do you cope with the workload?
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C024. order for food → order food
**Why:** "Order" is transitive and takes a direct object with no preposition.
**Examples:** Let's order food. / I ordered a coffee.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C025. request for help → request help / ask for help
**Why:** "Request" as a verb takes a direct object with no preposition; if you use "for," switch to "ask for."
**Examples:** We requested more time. / She asked for help with the report.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C026. good in English → good at English
**Why:** Skills and abilities take "good at," not "good in."
**Examples:** She is good at English. / He is good at solving problems.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C027. interested on → interested in
**Why:** The fixed collocation is "interested in" something.
**Examples:** I am interested in history. / Are you interested in joining us?
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C028. depend of → depend on
**Why:** The verb takes the preposition "on": you depend on someone or something.
**Examples:** It depends on the weather. / We depend on each other.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C029. discussed on → discussed
**Why:** "Discuss" is transitive and takes a direct object directly, with no "on" or "about."
**Examples:** We discussed the proposal. / They discussed the results in detail.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C030. listen music → listen to music
**Why:** "Listen" is intransitive and needs the preposition "to" before its object.
**Examples:** I listen to music while working. / Listen to me carefully.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C031. search it in Google → search for it on Google
**Why:** You "search for" a thing, and platforms like Google take "on."
**Examples:** I searched for it on Google. / Let me search for the address online.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C032. on next week → next week
**Why:** Time phrases with "next," "last," "this," and "every" take no preposition.
**Examples:** I will see you next week. / We are travelling next month.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C033. in Monday → on Monday
**Why:** Days of the week and dates take the preposition "on."
**Examples:** The meeting is on Monday. / She was born on the 5th of June.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C034. at morning → in the morning
**Why:** Parts of the day take "in the" (morning/afternoon/evening); "at" is reserved for "at night."
**Examples:** I exercise in the morning. / We will meet in the afternoon.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C035. in night → at night
**Why:** "Night" is the exception among parts of the day and takes "at."
**Examples:** I read at night. / It gets cold here at night.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C036. I reached to home → I reached home
**Why:** "Reach" is transitive and takes its destination directly; "home" also needs no preposition after motion verbs.
**Examples:** I reached home by eight. / We reached the airport early.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C037. I came to know → I found out / I learned
**Why:** "Came to know" is an Indian-English calque; native speakers say "found out" or "learned" for gaining information.
**Examples:** I found out about it yesterday. / I learned that the office was closed.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C038. do one thing → here's what we can do
**Why:** "Do one thing" as an opener is an Indian-English calque; native speakers introduce a suggestion with phrases like "here's what we can do."
**Examples:** Here's what we can do: send the file first. / How about this — let's call them directly.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C039. kindly do the needful → Could you please handle this
**Why:** "Do the needful" is dated, vague office jargon; a clear request states what you actually want done.
**Examples:** Could you please handle this? / Please take care of the rest.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C040. prepone → move earlier
**Why:** "Prepone" is an Indian coinage not used in standard English; say "move earlier" or "bring forward."
**Examples:** Can we move the meeting earlier? / They brought the deadline forward by a week.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C041. I am not getting you → I don't understand what you mean
**Why:** "Not getting you" is an Indian-English calque; the natural phrasing is "I don't understand."
**Examples:** I don't understand what you mean. / Sorry, I didn't quite follow that.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C042. What is your good name → What is your name
**Why:** "Good name" is a direct translation of a politeness formula; standard English just asks "What is your name?"
**Examples:** What is your name? / May I have your name, please?
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C043. passed out from college → graduated from college
**Why:** "Pass out" in standard English means to faint; for finishing studies, say "graduated."
**Examples:** I graduated from college in 2018. / She graduated with a degree in physics.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C044. out of station → out of town
**Why:** "Out of station" is an Indian-English calque; native speakers say "out of town" when away from home.
**Examples:** I'll be out of town next week. / He's out of town on business.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C045. I am staying in Kochi → I live in Kochi
**Why:** For your permanent home, use "live"; "stay" implies a temporary visit, like at a hotel.
**Examples:** I live in Kochi. / They live in a flat near the station.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C046. I stay with my parents → I live with my parents
**Why:** Permanent residence takes "live with"; "stay with" suggests a short visit.
**Examples:** I live with my parents. / She lives with two roommates.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C047. I am going to home → I am going home
**Why:** "Home" works as an adverb after motion verbs, so it takes no "to."
**Examples:** I am going home now. / He went home early.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C048. more better → better
**Why:** "Better" is already the comparative of "good," so adding "more" doubles the comparison.
**Examples:** This option is better. / I feel much better today.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C049. most easiest → easiest
**Why:** "Easiest" is already the superlative, so adding "most" doubles it.
**Examples:** This is the easiest way. / That was the easiest exam of all.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C050. less people → fewer people
**Why:** Use "fewer" for things you can count (people), and "less" for uncountable quantities (water, time).
**Examples:** Fewer people came than expected. / There were fewer mistakes this time.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C051. many informations → a lot of information
**Why:** "Information" is uncountable, so it has no plural and doesn't take "many"; use "a lot of" or "much."
**Examples:** I have a lot of information about this. / We need more information first.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C052. advices → advice
**Why:** "Advice" is uncountable and has no plural; for one item say "a piece of advice."
**Examples:** Thank you for the advice. / Let me give you a piece of advice.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C053. furnitures → furniture
**Why:** "Furniture" is uncountable and has no plural; count it as "pieces of furniture."
**Examples:** We bought new furniture. / There isn't much furniture in the room.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C054. equipments → equipment
**Why:** "Equipment" is uncountable and has no plural; count it as "pieces of equipment."
**Examples:** The lab has modern equipment. / We need more equipment for the trip.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C055. staffs → staff members
**Why:** "Staff" is a collective noun; for individuals say "staff members" or "members of staff."
**Examples:** All the staff members were present. / Two staff members are on leave.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C056. childrens → children
**Why:** "Children" is already the plural of "child," so it never takes another "-s."
**Examples:** The children are playing outside. / How many children do they have?
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C057. peoples → people
**Why:** "People" is already plural; "peoples" only means distinct ethnic groups or nations.
**Examples:** Many people attended. / People here are very friendly.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C058. I have 25 years → I am 25 years old
**Why:** In English you "are" an age, using "be," not "have" (which is a translation from other languages).
**Examples:** I am 25 years old. / She is ten years old.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C059. Myself Arun → I am Arun / My name is Arun
**Why:** "Myself Arun" is an Indian-English calque; introduce yourself with "I am" or "My name is."
**Examples:** I am Arun. / My name is Arun, and I work in sales.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C060. He is my cousin brother → He is my cousin
**Why:** "Cousin" already covers any cousin regardless of gender; "cousin brother/sister" is an Indian-English addition.
**Examples:** He is my cousin. / My cousin lives abroad.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C061. She is my real sister → She is my sister
**Why:** "Sister" already means a biological sibling; "real sister" is redundant in standard English.
**Examples:** She is my sister. / My sister is two years older than me.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C062. I and my friend → My friend and I
**Why:** Politeness convention puts the other person first, and "I" is correct because it is the subject.
**Examples:** My friend and I went shopping. / My colleague and I finished the report.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C063. Me and him went → He and I went
**Why:** As the subject of the verb, use the subject pronouns "he" and "I," not "me" and "him."
**Examples:** He and I went together. / She and I are friends.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C064. between you and I → between you and me
**Why:** After a preposition like "between," use object pronouns, so it must be "me," not "I."
**Examples:** This is between you and me. / There's no problem between you and me.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C065. I enjoyed a lot → I really enjoyed it
**Why:** "Enjoy" is transitive and needs an object ("it"); "I really enjoyed it" is the natural form.
**Examples:** I really enjoyed it. / We really enjoyed the trip.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C066. I am boring → I am bored
**Why:** The "-ing" form describes the cause ("this is boring"), while "-ed" describes how you feel.
**Examples:** I am bored. / The kids are bored at home.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C067. I felt very relax → I felt very relaxed
**Why:** "Relax" is a verb; to describe a feeling you need the "-ed" adjective "relaxed."
**Examples:** I felt very relaxed. / She looked relaxed after the holiday.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C068. I am confusing → I am confused
**Why:** "-ing" describes what causes the feeling ("the instructions are confusing"); "-ed" describes your own feeling.
**Examples:** I am confused. / He was confused by the directions.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C069. It is more easy → It is easier
**Why:** Short adjectives form the comparative with "-er," not "more"; "easy" becomes "easier."
**Examples:** It is easier than I thought. / This route is easier to follow.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C070. I did a mistake → I made a mistake
**Why:** Mistakes collocate with "make," not "do"; you make a mistake, an error, a decision.
**Examples:** I made a mistake. / Everyone makes mistakes sometimes.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C071. give an exam → take an exam
**Why:** The student "takes" an exam; the teacher "gives" or "sets" it. Reversing them is a common transfer error.
**Examples:** I take the exam next week. / She took three exams this term.
**Drill:** Say the wrong form once, the correct form three times, then make one new sentence.

## C072. write an exam → take an exam
**Why:** Standard usage is "take an exam" for sitting it; "write an exam" is regional.
