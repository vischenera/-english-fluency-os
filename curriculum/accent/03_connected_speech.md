# Accent Track 3 — Connected Speech

Native speech is not separate words — it's blended. Even with good sounds, speaking word-by-word marks you as a learner. These are the General American blending rules. Each card is a 20–40 second micro-drill: say the "slow/written" form once, then the "real/blended" form 3×.

How to hear a model (no files needed): search the phrase on **YouGlish** (US) or a **US text-to-speech**, then record & compare.

> Register note: these are for **casual, natural** speech — still fully intelligible, not slang. You'd use them in everyday conversation, interviews, and meetings. You'd only avoid them in very formal speeches or when stressing a word for emphasis.

## CS01. Contractions are the default
**What it is:** Americans almost always shrink "subject + verb" into one word; full forms sound stiff and over-careful.
**Written → Real:** I am ready  →  I'm ready
**More:** do not know → don't know · it is fine → it's fine · I would go → I'd go · you are late → you're late
**Micro-drill (20s):** Say the written form once, then the blended form 3× until it feels lazy and smooth.

## CS02. Gonna / wanna / gotta / hafta
**What it is:** Common verb-to-verb pairs collapse: going to → gonna, want to → wanna, got to → gotta, have to → hafta.
**Written → Real:** I am going to call  →  I'm gonna call
**More:** I want to go → I wanna go · I've got to leave → I gotta leave · I have to work → I hafta work
**Micro-drill (20s):** Say the written form once, then the blended form 3× until it feels lazy and smooth.

## CS03. Flap T (the signature GenAm sound)
**What it is:** A T or D between two vowels turns into a quick D-like tap of the tongue — light and fast, not a full "t."
**Written → Real:** water  →  wadder
**More:** better → bedder · got it → goddit · a lot of → a lodda
**Micro-drill (20s):** Say the written form once, then the blended form 3× until it feels lazy and smooth.

## CS04. Linking consonant → vowel
**What it is:** When a word ends in a consonant and the next starts with a vowel, carry the consonant onto the next word.
**Written → Real:** an apple  →  a-napple
**More:** pick it up → pi-ki-dup · turn it on → tur-ni-don
**Micro-drill (20s):** Say the written form once, then the blended form 3× until it feels lazy and smooth.

## CS05. Linking vowel → vowel (tiny glide)
**What it is:** When one word ends in a vowel and the next begins with one, slide between them with a tiny /w/ or /y/ glide — no hard stop.
**Written → Real:** go on  →  go-won
**More:** I am → I-yam · do it → do-wit · she always → she-yalways
**Micro-drill (20s):** Say the written form once, then the blended form 3× until it feels lazy and smooth.

## CS06. "You" becomes "ya" / "juh"
**What it is:** Unstressed "you" weakens to "ya," and a preceding d/t often fuses with it into a "j" or "ch" sound.
**Written → Real:** did you  →  didja
**More:** what do you → whaddaya · don't you → doncha · got you → gotcha
**Micro-drill (20s):** Say the written form once, then the blended form 3× until it feels lazy and smooth.

## CS07. Reduced function words (schwa)
**What it is:** Small grammar words (of, for, and, to, can, your) lose their full vowel and shrink to a quick "uh"-type sound.
**Written → Real:** a cup of tea  →  a cuppa tea
**More:** for a while → fer a while · bread and butter → bread-n butter · I want to → I wanna · I can go → I kn go · your turn → yer turn
**Micro-drill (20s):** Say the written form once, then the blended form 3× until it feels lazy and smooth.

## CS08. H-dropping in weak pronouns
**What it is:** In unstressed him/her/them after a verb, the H disappears and the pronoun links onto the word before it.
**Written → Real:** tell him  →  tell'im
**More:** ask her → ask'er · give them → give'em
**Micro-drill (20s):** Say the written form once, then the blended form 3× until it feels lazy and smooth.

## CS09. Held / dropped final T
**What it is:** A final or medial T often isn't released — it's held silently or swallowed, especially after N or before another consonant.
**Written → Real:** important  →  impor-nt (T not released)
**More:** can't go → "can'(t) go" (T held silent, no puff) · first thing → firs thing
**Micro-drill (20s):** Say the written form once, then the blended form 3× until it feels lazy and smooth.

## CS10. Assimilation (neighbors blend)
**What it is:** Sounds reshape to match their neighbors — t+y fuses to "ch/j," and n borrows the place of the next sound.
**Written → Real:** would you  →  wouldja
**More:** don't you → doncha · in the → in-nuh · ten minutes → tem-minutes
**Micro-drill (20s):** Say the written form once, then the blended form 3× until it feels lazy and smooth.
