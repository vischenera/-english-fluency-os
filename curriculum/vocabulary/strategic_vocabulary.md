# Strategic Chunks & Collocations

Fluency lives in chunks, not single words. These are high-frequency multi-word pieces you can retrieve whole, so you build sentences faster with fewer pauses. Don't memorize silently — say each one inside a real sentence about your life or work.

## Decisions & Work

## V001. make a decision
**Means:** to decide something
**Example:** We need to make a decision about the budget before Friday.
**Drill:** Say a true sentence about a decision you made this week.

## V002. take responsibility for
**Means:** to accept that something is your job or your fault (followed by a noun or -ing)
**Example:** I'll take responsibility for the report and send it out today.
**Drill:** Say one thing you take responsibility for at work or home.

## V003. come up with
**Means:** to think of or produce an idea or plan
**Example:** Can you come up with a few names for the new project?
**Drill:** Say a true sentence about an idea you came up with recently.

## V005. sort it out

**Drill:** Say something you need to sort out this week.

## V006. deal with

## V007. look into it

## V010. push back the deadline

## Opinion & Discourse

**Means:** used to introduce a frank or true opinion

**Drill:** Say a true opinion starting with "To be honest...".

## V022. the thing is
**Means:** used to introduce a key point or problem
**Example:** I'd love to come, but the thing is, I'm already busy that day.
**Drill:** Say a sentence starting with "The thing is...".

## V028. on second thought
**Means:** after reconsidering; having changed your mind
**Example:** On second thought, let's not go out tonight.
**Drill:** Say a sentence where you change your mind with "On second thought".

## V029. at the end of the day
**Means:** when everything is considered; ultimately

**Drill:** Say a true sentence starting with "At the end of the day...".

## V042. a lot of

**Example:** There's a lot of work to do before the launch.
**Drill:** Say a true sentence using "a lot of".

## V048. look forward to

**Means:** to be expected or required to do something (followed by a verb)

**Example:** I'll be there for you no matter what.

## Social & Repair

**Drill:** Say a sentence using "never mind" to drop a point.

**Means:** an informal way to admit a small mistake
**Example:** My bad — I sent it to the wrong person.

## V061. fair enough
**Means:** that's a reasonable point; I accept that

## Problem & Cause

## V069. that's why
**Means:** that is the reason; introduces a result
**Example:** The train was late — that's why I missed the meeting.
**Drill:** Explain a result with "that's why".

## V070. due to
**Means:** because of (followed by a noun)
**Example:** The flight was delayed due to bad weather.
**Drill:** Say a true sentence using "due to".

## V071. lead to
**Means:** to cause or result in something (followed by a noun)
**Example:** Skipping breakfast can lead to low energy.
**Drill:** Say something that can "lead to" a result.

## V072. result in
**Means:** to cause a particular outcome (followed by a noun)
**Example:** The mistake resulted in a big delay.
**Drill:** Say a sentence where one thing "results in" another.

## V073. on purpose
**Means:** deliberately; intentionally
**Example:** I didn't break it on purpose — it was an accident.
**Drill:** Say a true sentence using "on purpose".

## V074. by mistake
**Means:** accidentally; not on purpose
**Example:** I deleted the file by mistake.
**Drill:** Say something you did "by mistake".

## V075. work out (well)
**Means:** to happen successfully in the end
**Example:** Everything worked out well in the end.
**Drill:** Say a true sentence about something that worked out.

## V076. end up with
**Means:** to finally have something (often unplanned; followed by a noun)
**Example:** We ended up with way too much food at the party.
**Drill:** Say a sentence about what you ended up with.

## V077. be stuck on
**Means:** to be unable to make progress with something (followed by a noun)

**Drill:** Say something you're stuck on right now.

## V078. make progress
**Means:** to move forward or improve toward a goal
**Example:** I'm slowly making progress on my English.
**Drill:** Say a true sentence about progress you're making.
